# THE PSYCHOLOGY OF JAPA — full script (draft 1)

Format: hand-drawn psychology explainer, same as the pilot (`psych/japa_pilot.py`).
One drawing per line, calm narrator, 3-word karaoke captions.
Length: ~1,350 spoken words ≈ 9 minutes at the pilot's pace.

Tone rules
- Never shame the ones who leave, never shame the ones who stay.
- Explain feelings with real psychology ideas, said in plain words.
- Every number is attributed and checked before recording (see **Facts to verify** at the bottom). Lines marked ⚠ contain a number.

Each line: **spoken text**, then *[drawing]*.

## CHAPTER 1 — THE MIRROR

13. **In 1954, a psychologist called Leon Festinger noticed something simple.**
    *[old-style scientist at a desk, "1954"]*
14. **We don't measure our lives against a perfect standard. We measure them against the people around us.**
    *[figure holding a ruler up to another figure]*
15. **He called it social comparison.**
    *[two figures side by side, "SOCIAL COMPARISON" label]*
16. **For most of history, the people around you lived on your street.**
    *[small street, houses, neighbours chatting]*
17. **Now they live in your phone.**
    *[same figure, phone glowing, tiny people inside]*
18. **Your classmate in Manchester. Your cousin in Toronto. The guy from church, now in Berlin.**
    *[three photo frames: rain, snow, a train]*
19. **You see the new coat, the clean train, the first payslip in pounds.**
    *[coat, train, payslip with £]*
20. **You don't see the night shifts, the rent, or the loneliness.**
    *[same photo, the edges peeled back: night shift, bills, empty room]*
21. **Psychologists have a name for that gap: relative deprivation.**
    *["RELATIVE DEPRIVATION" label, two stacks of blocks, one taller]*
22. **It's not that your life got worse. It's that the picture of a "normal" life moved, and moved far away.**
    *[goalpost being carried onto a plane]*

---

## CHAPTER 2 — THE CEILING

23. **But comparison alone doesn't make someone sell everything and leave.**
    *[figure shrugging next to a phone]*
24. **Something deeper is going on. Something about effort.**
    *[figure pushing a heavy boulder]*
25. **Think about a normal week.**
    *[weekly calendar]*
26. **The light goes. The generator needs fuel. The price of fuel changed again.**
    *[bulb off, generator, fuel pump with a rising arrow]*
27. **You saved for a year, and the naira lost value while you were saving.**
    *[piggy bank with a slow leak]*
28. **You did everything right, and the outcome still wasn't yours to control.**
    *[figure at a slot machine]*
29. **Psychologists call this an external locus of control: the feeling that what happens to you is decided somewhere else.**
    *[figure with strings attached to a hand above the clouds]*
30. **And when that feeling lasts long enough, people stop trying to fix the system.**
    *[figure sitting down next to the boulder]*
31. **They start looking for a different system.**
    *[same figure looking up at a door in the sky]*
32. **That's why so many people who japa will tell you the same thing:**
    *[several faces, speech bubbles]*
33. **It's not just the money. It's wanting a life where if you work hard, it actually adds up.**
    *[stairs that go up, step by step, labelled "effort → result"]*

---

## CHAPTER 3 — THE DECISION

34. **Deciding to go isn't one moment. It's hundreds of small ones.**
    *[path made of stepping stones]*
35. **The first IELTS registration.**
    *[exam paper, "IELTS", pencil]*
36. **The first time you Google "proof of funds."**
    *[search bar "proof of funds"]*
37. **The family meeting where an uncle says, "Go. Go now, while you're young."**
    *[circle of family on chairs, uncle pointing at the door]*
38. **In many Nigerian homes, japa isn't one person's dream. It's the family's investment.**
    *[family putting coins into one suitcase]*
39. **⚠ And it pays back. Nigerians abroad send home billions of dollars every year — more than many of the country's biggest industries.**
    *[arrows of money flying from planes back to houses]*
40. **That means the person leaving carries two loads: their own hopes, and everyone else's.**
    *[figure carrying a suitcase plus a house on their back]*
41. **There's another trap here. The more you've spent preparing, the harder it is to change your mind.**
    *[stack of receipts growing taller]*
42. **Psychologists call it the sunk cost effect.**
    *["SUNK COST" label, coins sinking into water]*
43. **After the exams, the fees, the visa, the goodbye party... turning back can feel impossible.**
    *[bridge burning behind the figure]*

---

## CHAPTER 4 — THE ARRIVAL

44. **Then the plane lands.**
    *[plane touching down, grey sky]*
45. **And for many, the first feeling isn't joy. It's cold.**
    *[figure in a thin jacket, snowflakes]*
46. **The first months are hard in ways the pictures never show.**
    *[small room, one bed, suitcase still packed]*
47. **⚠ Some take jobs far below their qualifications while they re-certify, re-train, or wait for papers.**
    *[figure in a delivery uniform, a framed degree on the wall behind]*
48. **The voice notes from home come in at 3 a.m.**
    *[phone buzzing on a dark nightstand, clock 3:00]*
49. **You miss a wedding. A naming ceremony. A funeral.**
    *[three empty chairs with name cards]*
50. **Psychologist Pauline Boss has a name for this: ambiguous loss.**
    *["AMBIGUOUS LOSS" label]*
51. **The people you love aren't gone. But you're not there, either.**
    *[figure on one side of a sea, family on the other, a phone between them]*
52. **And then there's the strangest feeling of all: you're foreign there, and becoming foreign at home.**
    *[figure standing on a line between two maps, belonging to neither]*
53. **But slowly, for many, it gets better.**
    *[same room, now with plants and photos on the wall]*
54. **The payslip arrives. The light stays on. The plan stops loading.**
    *[payslip, steady bulb, progress bar at 100%]*

---

## CHAPTER 5 — THE ONES WHO STAY

55. **Now let's go back home.**
    *[plane flying the other way, Lagos skyline]*
56. **For every person who leaves, there's a hospital ward with one less nurse.**
    *[ward with an empty nurse's station]*
57. **⚠ A doctor now covering the shifts of two.**
    *[doctor with two shadows, both tired]*
58. **A friend group that keeps getting smaller.**
    *[group photo where people fade out one by one]*
59. **The ones who stay feel things too.**
    *[figure alone at a table set for six]*
60. **Some feel left behind. Some feel judged, as if staying means you failed.**
    *[figure under a big spotlight, "still here?"]*
61. **But staying is also a choice.**
    *[figure planting a seed]*
62. **People stay to build companies, to care for parents, to fix the things that pushed everyone else away.**
    *[small shop, an old parent, a figure with a toolbox]*
63. **And not everyone who leaves stays gone.**
    *[plane flying home]*
64. **Some come back with new skills, new money, new ideas. Researchers call it brain circulation.**
    *["BRAIN CIRCULATION" label, arrows looping between two countries]*

## ENDING

65. **So, is japa about money?**
    *[the naira note from the first scene, back again]*
66. **Partly. But underneath the money is something more human.**
    *[note folds into a heart]*
67. **The need to feel that your effort matters.**
    *[the staircase from Chapter 2]*
68. **That your future is something you can plan, not something that happens to you.**
    *[figure drawing their own path with a pencil]*
69. **Some people look for that somewhere else. Some people stay and try to build it here.**
    *[split screen: figure boarding a plane / figure building a house]*
70. **Both of them want the same thing.**
    *[both figures look up at the same star]*
71. **A country where nobody has to run.**
    *[figure standing still, smiling, sunrise]*

# HOW DANGOTE BECAME THE RICHEST MAN IN AFRICA, EXPLAINED LIKE YOU'RE 5: script (draft 1)

Format: bold-outline cartoon explainer, like the Rockefeller reference. About 8 minutes.

**Education only. Not financial advice.** Every number is reported, attributed and checked (see the list at the end).

---

## COLD OPEN
1. **Imagine a little boy in Kano, selling sweets to other children.**
2. **Now imagine that same boy, grown up, owning a refinery that can process six hundred and fifty thousand barrels of oil every single day.**
3. **This is the story of Aliko Dangote, the man Forbes has ranked as the richest person in Africa for more than a decade.**
4. **And here is the surprising part: his story is not about luck.**
5. **It is about a few simple ideas, so simple that a five-year-old can follow them.**
6. **Let's begin.**

## CHAPTER 1: THE BOY FROM KANO
7. **Aliko Dangote was born in 1957, in Kano, in the north of Nigeria.**
8. **He came from a family of traders. His great-grandfather, Alhassan Dantata, was one of the wealthiest merchants in West Africa.**
9. **So business was not a subject at school. It was the air he breathed at home.**
10. **As a boy, he reportedly bought sweets in bulk and sold them to other children, and made a small profit.**
11. **That is the first big idea: buy for less, sell for more. Every business in the world is a grown-up version of that.**
12. **Later, he studied business at Al-Azhar University in Cairo.**
13. **Then he came home, with a plan.**

## CHAPTER 2: THE LOAN
14. **In 1977, Dangote moved to Lagos.**
15. **He had ideas, but not much money. So he asked his uncle for help.**
16. **According to widely reported accounts, his uncle lent him about five hundred thousand naira.**
17. **With that loan, he started trading basic goods: things like sugar, rice and cement.**
18. **Everybody needs food and building materials. So there is always a customer.**
19. **In 1981, he set up the company that would grow into the Dangote Group.**
20. **Lesson two: start with something people always need, even if you start small.**

## CHAPTER 3: THE BIG IDEA
21. **Now here is where it gets interesting.**
22. **Imagine you sell lemonade. But you buy your lemons from someone else.**
23. **If that person raises the price, your lemonade gets expensive. If he stops selling, your business stops.**
24. **So what if you grew the lemons yourself?**
25. **That is the idea behind what experts call backward integration: instead of only selling things, you make them.**
26. **In the early 2000s, the Nigerian government pushed companies to produce more at home instead of importing.**
27. **Dangote saw the moment, and moved from trading to manufacturing.**
28. **Sugar. Flour. Salt. And the big one: cement.**
29. **Lesson three: the person who makes the product often keeps more of the profit.**

## CHAPTER 4: THE CEMENT KINGDOM
30. **For years, Nigeria imported a large share of its cement.**
31. **Dangote built a huge cement plant in Obajana, in Kogi State. It began operating in 2007.**
32. **In 2010, Dangote Cement was listed on the Nigerian stock exchange, so ordinary investors could buy a share of it.**
33. **Later, the company spread to other African countries too.**
34. **More cement made in Africa meant fewer imports, more jobs, and more money staying in the region.**
35. **Cement became the engine that made Dangote one of the richest men on the continent.**

## CHAPTER 5: THE GIANT BET
36. **But Dangote wanted to go bigger.**
37. **Nigeria is one of Africa's biggest oil producers. Yet for years, it exported crude oil, and imported a lot of its petrol.**
38. **Think about that. Sell the raw lemons cheap, and buy the lemonade back expensive.**
39. **So Dangote decided to build a refinery, in Lekki, near Lagos.**
40. **It was designed to process six hundred and fifty thousand barrels of crude oil a day.**
41. **It reportedly cost around twenty billion dollars, and it took far longer than planned, with delays and disputes along the way.**
42. **Many people doubted it would ever work.**
43. **In 2024, the refinery began producing petrol and diesel.**
44. **Lesson four: very big plans can take a very long time. Patience is part of the price.**

## CHAPTER 6: HERO OR VILLAIN?
45. **Now, a fair story must show both sides.**
46. **Many people admire Dangote for building factories, creating jobs, and making goods at home.**
47. **But critics say that when one group becomes this big, it can have too much power over prices and competition.**
48. **Both of these views are part of the real story.**
49. **And it is a good reminder: success on this scale brings attention, and questions, too.**

## CHAPTER 7: THE MONEY RULES
50. **So what can an ordinary person learn from all this?**
51. **One: start small, with something people always need.**
52. **Two: make or build things, don't only buy and sell.**
53. **Three: learn your whole chain, from the raw materials to the customer.**
54. **Four: think in years, not weeks.**
55. **Five: big bets carry big risks, so never gamble money you cannot afford to lose.**
56. **You don't need billions to use these ideas. You can use them on a small shop, a skill, or a side business.**

## ENDING
57. **From sweets in Kano to a refinery in Lagos.**
58. **The tools were simple: buy for less, sell for more, make what you sell, and be patient.**
59. **Which of these rules will you use first? Tell us in the comments.**

# HOW FOLORUNSHO ALAKIJA BECAME ONE OF AFRICA'S RICHEST WOMEN (EXPLAINED LIKE YOU'RE 5): script (draft 1)

Format: bold-outline cartoon explainer, same as the Dangote video. About 6 minutes.

**Education only. Facts are public and hedged ('reportedly', 'according to reports'). No net-worth figures.**

---

## COLD OPEN
1. **Imagine a young woman in Lagos, working as a secretary.**
2. **She types letters, answers phones, and takes notes for other people's dreams.**
3. **Decades later, she is on lists of Africa's richest women.**
4. **This is the story of Folorunsho Alakija, explained like you're five.**
5. **And her secret was not luck. It was a few simple ideas.**

## CHAPTER 1: THE SECRETARY
6. **Folorunsho Alakija was born in 1951, in Nigeria.**
7. **She went to London to study, and she trained as a secretary.**
8. **Back in Nigeria, she worked as an executive secretary at several companies, including a bank.**
9. **Many people would have stayed there, safe and steady.**
10. **But she was watching, and learning how business really works.**
11. **Lesson one: every job is a classroom, if you pay attention.**

## CHAPTER 2: THE SEWING MACHINE
12. **Then she did something brave. She studied fashion design, and started her own tailoring label.**
13. **She called it Supreme Stitches.**
14. **Her dresses were for weddings, parties and big occasions.**
15. **Her clients reportedly included wives of well-known and powerful people.**
16. **Here is a small idea with a big effect: when people trust your work, they tell others, and they tell important people.**
17. **Lesson two: do one thing so well that people talk about it.**
18. **Later, the label was renamed The Rose of Sharon House of Fashion.**

## CHAPTER 3: THE OIL BLOCK
19. **Now, imagine a door opens.**
20. **In the early 1990s, the government was giving out oil licences, and she applied.**
21. **According to reports, she received an oil block through her company, Famfa Oil.**
22. **People were surprised. A fashion designer in oil?**
23. **Some people asked how she got it. The story is told in different ways, and we only know what has been publicly reported.**
24. **What is clear is what she did next.**
25. **She didn't have oil rigs, ships, or a team of engineers. So she found partners who did.**
26. **Lesson three: you don't need to own every skill. You need to know who to partner with.**

## CHAPTER 4: THE BIG FIELD
27. **The partnership brought in oil companies, including Texaco, which later became part of Chevron.**
28. **Together, they developed a huge offshore oil field called Agbami.**
29. **It took many years, and a lot of money, and it was risky.**
30. **Oil didn't flow overnight. It took patience, years of work, and hope.**
31. **When the oil started coming out, it became one of the biggest stories in Nigerian business.**
32. **Over the years, Forbes has listed her among the richest women in Africa.**
33. **Lesson four: big things take a long time. Patience is part of the price.**

## CHAPTER 5: THE FAIR VIEW
34. **Like many oil stories, hers has been talked about, and debated.**
35. **Some people admire her for opening a door in a business that few women entered.**
36. **Others ask questions about how oil licences were given out in Nigeria in those days.**
37. **A fair story shows both sides, and then asks what we can learn.**

## CHAPTER 6: GIVING BACK
38. **She also started the Rose of Sharon Foundation, which supports widows and orphans.**
39. **Lesson five: when you win, make room for others.**

## CHAPTER 7: THE RULES
40. **So what can you take from her story?**
41. **One: learn from your job, whatever it is.**
42. **Two: build a skill people trust.**
43. **Three: partner with people who have what you lack.**
44. **Four: be patient with big goals.**
45. **Five: give back.**
46. **You can use these on a small shop, a skill, or a side business.**

## ENDING
47. **From typing letters to a giant oil field.**
48. **Which of these five lessons will you use first? Tell us in the comments.**
49. **And subscribe to Naija Untold for the next story.**

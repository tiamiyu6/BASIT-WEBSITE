# THE VIEW MACHINE: the pattern behind three videos from one small channel

Source: three videos from one channel with about 2,100 subscribers, opened and described in Chrome (numbers as reported on 3 Nov 2026).

| Video | Views | Age | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| How Did Humans Start Using Cocaine? | 210,745 | about 1 month | 13:07 |
| How Did Humans Invent the Engine? | 349,486 | about 2 weeks | 20:17 |
| How Did Humans Invent the Cell Phone? | 8,749 | 2 days | 22:23 |

A channel this small getting 200,000 to 350,000 views means the **format** is doing the work, not the audience. That is the part we copy. We copy the pattern, never their art, characters, wording or exact topics.

## The 8 parts of the pattern

1. **Title:** `How Did Humans [Start Using / Invent] [everyday thing]?` Always an origin question about something the viewer knows.
2. **Thumbnail:** two words only, "FIRST ___?", big yellow text with a black outline across the top. One goofy cartoon character doing something with the object, one shocked second character at the right. Bright flat colours, one clear object. Same template every time, so the channel looks like one brand.
3. **First 30 seconds:** a calm, ordinary scene in the second person ("you pick up your phone about 100 times a day"), then a small mystery or twist ("it is made of rocks, literally"). The title question is asked out loud inside the first minute.
4. **Length and chapters:** 13 to 22 minutes, 8 chapters in a fixed order: the hook, the roots, the early use, the turning point, the science, the risks or limits, the big consequence, the legacy.
5. **Pace:** about **33 to 37 new pictures per minute**, which is one picture every 1.7 seconds, all static. Ours is about 5 per minute, so this is the biggest gap.
6. **Ending:** return to the opening scene, one closing line that lands ("the plant never changed, we did"), then a tease of the next video and an end-screen card pointing to it. Every video feeds the next one.
7. **Description:** a plain disclaimer, a two-paragraph summary, a short list of real sources (Wikipedia and journals), a corrections section when something was wrong, a "which part surprised you most and what should we trace next?" line, then hashtags and a people-mentioned card.
8. **Comments:** the channel itself posts the first comment, admits mistakes openly (this got the most likes), and replies to requests for the next topic. That is free engagement.

## What we do differently today, and the fix

| Today | The pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 4 to 6 minute videos | 13 to 22 minutes | Step up to 8 to 10 minutes, then 12 or more once the machine works |
| 5 pictures a minute | 33 to 37 a minute | See "The picture problem" below |
| Burned-in captions | None (YouTube's own captions) | Keep ours for TikTok, drop them on YouTube |
| Topics: tycoons, one-off stories | One repeatable question format | One topic question per video, in the same title and thumbnail template |
| No next-video chain | End card to the next video | Plan topics in pairs, end each one with a tease and a card |

## The picture problem (and the answer)

A 10-minute video at 30 pictures a minute needs 300 shots. We can't ask ChatGPT for 300 pictures one at a time.

The answer is to multiply each generated picture:
- **4 scenes in one picture:** ask ChatGPT for a 2 by 2 grid of four related scenes in one image. I cut it into four pictures. This is the cheapest gain, but each piece is only about 836 pixels wide, so we test one first to see if it looks sharp enough.
- **3 shots from each picture:** a wide shot, a close-up on the character's face, and a close-up on the object. These count as different shots in an edit.
- Together that is about 12 shots per generation, so a 10-minute video is roughly 25 to 30 generations. That is the same effort as one of our current videos.

## The checklist for every new video

1. Pick one everyday thing. Check no one has a big video on exactly that.
2. Write the title as `How Did Humans ___?` and the thumbnail as `FIRST ___?`.
3. Write the second-person opening: ordinary scene, then the twist, then the question out loud.
4. Plan 8 chapters in the fixed order.
5. Write the script in simple words, and list every fact with a source in a table.
6. Fact-check the table before the voice is made. Move anything doubtful to "reportedly", or cut it.
7. Make the pictures with the Claude panel (grids and close-ups), then build the video.
8. Upload with the description pattern, the corrections section if needed, and altered-content set to **Yes**.
9. Post the first comment ourselves, pin it, and reply to every topic request.
10. End with a tease and an end card to the next video.

## Rules to keep us safe
- Copy the pattern, not their pictures, wording, characters or exact topics.
- Always set altered content to **Yes**. Always say "reportedly" for anything doubtful.
- Education only. No claims about living people that we can't source.
- The channel we studied had to correct its own mistakes in the description. We fact-check first so we don't have to.
